New / NaviSyncRock

Your Navidrome, on a real iPod.

Sync songs, albums, artists, and playlists from your server to a Rockbox player: your server transcodes, the album art actually renders even on old hardware, playlists are written for you, and plays on the iPod sync back to your server as scrobbles. Two custom NaviBeat themes ride along. A Mac add-on.

Built on Rockbox, a wonderful volunteer-run open-source project that has kept old iPods playing for decades. We’re fans building on top of their work, not affiliated with the Rockbox project, and glad it exists.

Click wheel, meet self-hosting.

Say it, hear it.

Your server. Your library. Your rules.

NaviBeat is a client. Your music lives where it already does: a Raspberry Pi, a Synology, a home server under someone’s desk. We never see your library, your credentials, or a single play event. We couldn’t if we wanted to: the app has no backend. The only services NaviBeat ever talks to are the ones you sign in to: your Navidrome server, and optionally Last.fm. Read the privacy policy.

FAQ

Questions you were going to ask anyway.

NaviBeat is a native Apple-ecosystem music client for self-hosted Navidrome, Airsonic-Advanced, Gonic, and OpenSubsonic servers. It runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV, and Apple Watch, plus CarPlay, and is one $5.99 one-time Universal Purchase. It is a client only: it has no backend, no analytics, and no tracking, and connects solely to the server you run.
NaviBeat is a single native app that spans all five Apple platforms, iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV, and Apple Watch, plus CarPlay, for one $5.99 Universal Purchase. It is built in SwiftUI for each device rather than a wrapped web view, supports Navidrome multi-library switching server-side, ships time-synced lyrics, internet radio, offline pinning, and Server Health diagnostics, and has no backend, no analytics, and no tracking. It works with Navidrome, Airsonic-Advanced, Gonic, and any OpenSubsonic server.
Yes. NaviBeat is a client: it plays music from a server you run. Navidrome is free, open source, and runs on anything from a Raspberry Pi to a NAS. Any OpenSubsonic-compatible server works too (Gonic, Airsonic-Advanced). navidrome.org takes about 15 minutes with Docker. NaviBeat is also officially listed on the navidrome.org/apps directory with the OpenSubsonic indicator.
Yes. NaviBeat is a Universal Purchase. Buy once on any Apple device and it installs on the other four (Mac, Apple TV, iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch) at no extra cost. The Apple Watch app ships embedded in the iPhone app, so it installs automatically when you pair your Watch.
Yes. NaviBeat is live on the App Store on all five Apple platforms: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV, and Apple Watch. One $5.99 Universal Purchase covers every device, with no subscription. Open the App Store listing →
Yes. Pin individual albums, playlists, or whole artists for offline playback. NaviBeat keeps an LRU cache with a size limit you set in Settings. Flights, subways, and spotty hotel Wi-Fi don’t stop the music. Downloads sync across devices via your server, not our cloud.
Yes. NaviBeat is built for self-hosters who lock their server down. On the connection screen you can add custom HTTP headers for Cloudflare Access (your CF-Access-Client-Id and CF-Access-Client-Secret) or Authelia, attach an mTLS client certificate, or simply reach your server over Tailscale. It all works on every platform, with no third-party relay in between.
A diagnostics screen for self-hosters. It shows your server type and version, the OpenSubsonic extensions it advertises, the library scan status and track count, and whether artist images, lyrics and album covers are resolving, so you can confirm your Navidrome is set up exactly the way you expect.
Yes. NaviBeat detects multi-library servers automatically and surfaces a picker: at the top of the Library tab on iPhone, in the sidebar on iPad and Mac, and as a pill row on Apple TV. Pick All Libraries, a single library, or any subset. Filtering is server-side, so pagination and ranking stay intact, and the picker is hidden on single-library servers. See the multi-library page for the full story.

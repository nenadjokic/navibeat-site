Every feature, one page

Everything NaviBeat does.

NaviBeat is a native music player for your own Navidrome or Subsonic server, on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV and Apple Watch, plus CarPlay, for one $5.99 purchase. This is the whole list: what is on the App Store today, and what is finished and running in the beta right now.

In the beta right now

Finished, running, and one release away.

Everything below is built and in testers' hands through TestFlight today. It reaches the App Store in the next release. Anyone can join the beta, and it is free.

Beta / Offline

Offline stopped being a separate mode.

Downloaded music lives inside the normal app. The same Home, the same artists, the same albums, with anything you do not have on the device shown greyed out instead of hidden, so you always know what is missing rather than wondering where it went.

When your server cannot be reached, NaviBeat switches to your downloads on its own and switches back when the server returns. Favourites, ratings and artwork keep working while you are cut off, and catch up afterwards.

Beta / Player

Jump buttons, either side of Play.

Skip back and forward by 10, 15 or 30 seconds. Look at the row in the screenshot: the jump pair sits either side of Play and Next and Previous are still there, which is the part other players get wrong.

They can be limited to one library, which is the point if your audiobooks sit on the same server as your music: turn them on there and your music player is unchanged. Off until you ask for them.

iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV.

Beta / Appearance

Pick your own accent colour.

Six one-tap presets, a full colour picker, and a hex field if you know exactly what you want. Amber is still the default and one tap brings it back.

The text and glyphs drawn on top of your colour switch between black and white on their own, so a pale yellow stays as readable as a deep purple, and the gradient follows your choice instead of staying orange.

Beta / Continuity

Start on one device, carry on with another.

That card at the top of Home is another device playing right now. It names the device, shows the track and how far in it is, and one tap picks the whole queue up where it stands. Tap it and the other device pauses itself, so you are never chased through the house by your own music.

It runs through your own server's play queue, so there is nothing extra to configure and nothing leaves your network.

Beta / Playlists

Smart Playlists you write yourself.

Build a playlist out of rules rather than by hand: artist, genre, year, rating, play count, when you added it, whether it is in another playlist, and more. Results update as your library does, and you see them while you are still editing the rules. Definitions sync across your devices, and a snapshot can be written back to your server as an ordinary playlist so every other client sees it too.

Beta / Servers

Saved servers, and no retyping.

Keep several servers side by side and switch between them without entering anything again. Each entry carries its own proxy headers and its own mTLS client certificate, so a server behind an identity-aware proxy is a saved entry like any other.

Beta / Artists

A discography that behaves like a discography.

Albums grouped by release type, so albums, EPs, singles, live records and compilations stop fighting for the same list. An All Songs view flattens an artist's whole catalogue into one sortable list, and albums released in the same year are finally in the right order: if your files carry a release date, NaviBeat reads it down to the day, and an original release beats a later reissue.

Beta / Lyrics

An optional lyrics lookup, off until you ask.

If your server and its own lyrics plugin both come up empty, NaviBeat can ask lrclib.net instead. That lookup is off until you turn it on, and it sends the song title, artist, album and length. Nothing about you and nothing about your server.

Beta / Automation

Shortcuts, sharing and accessibility.

Shortcuts actions for play, pause, next, previous and shuffle, plus Play Playlist by name. Copy Share Link asks your server for a proper share URL rather than pasting anything of yours into a message. Every transport control announces what it does to VoiceOver, and Reduce Motion turns off the animation the rest of the app leans on.

On the App Store today

Your library, the way you filed it.

Browsing

Albums, artists, genres, playlists, folders.

Sort and filter the way you think: by name, by artist, by year, by date added, by rating, by play count. Genres are first class, folder browsing is there for libraries organised by directory, and every tag on an album renders as a chip you can tap through. Compilations and album artists are handled properly rather than collapsed.

Multi-Library

Real Navidrome multi-library support.

All Libraries, one, or any subset you like, filtered on the server rather than hidden in the app. Music in one library and audiobooks in another stay apart everywhere: Home, search, shuffle, CarPlay and the Watch. How it works.

No other Apple client filters by musicFolder.

Library / Home

A Home screen made of your own listening.

Recently played, most played, recently added, favourites, On This Day, and shelves built from what you actually return to. Star ratings and favourites sync straight to your server, so they are the same in every other client you use.

Nothing recommended by a stranger.

Library / Stats

Listening stats that stay on your side.

A 24 hour listening clock, a weekday heatmap, and your top artists, albums and songs with a week, month or year switcher. Computed from your own play history, not from a service.

iPhone, iPad and Mac.

On the App Store today

Playback and sound.

Lyrics

Lyrics that keep time.

Time-synced LRC lyrics on every platform, scrolling with the song, with a tap on any line to seek there. Plain lyrics render properly when that is all a track has. The Watch gets them too.

Sound / Path

A real audio path.

A selectable high fidelity playback engine, a 10 band equalizer with presets, ReplayGain per track or per album or automatic, and gapless playback for the records that were written to be heard that way.

FLAC, ALAC, MP3, AAC, Opus, Ogg and more.

Sound / Quality

Wi-Fi and cellular, set separately.

Ten tiers, from the original file down to a light Opus stream, chosen independently for Wi-Fi and for mobile data. Auto sends the original at home and something kind to your data plan on the road. Your server does the transcoding, so nothing is re-encoded on the phone.

A codec and bitrate badge tells you what you are hearing, and can be turned off.

Sound / Queue

The queue, and what happens when it ends.

Reorder, shuffle, repeat, play next, add to queue, and a sleep timer. AutoMix keeps going when the queue runs out, using similar tracks resolved from your server or from Last.fm and ListenBrainz if you connect them. Internet radio stations too.

On the App Store today

Offline, properly.

Downloads

Pin a song, an album, a playlist, or an entire artist.

A download queue with live speed, a history, and one tap to retry whatever failed. Artwork and metadata are cached with the audio, so the library browses offline as well as plays, which is the part most clients skip.

Prefer Downloaded Music plays the local file even when the server is reachable. Keep Streamed Songs quietly saves what you play. Downloads you asked for are never deleted automatically.

Linux and beyond

Linux now, Android and Windows next.

A native Linux desktop build is out as a free open beta, carrying its own Java and VLC so there is nothing to install. Optional, always

What it connects to, if you let it.

Rockbox

NaviSyncRock: your Navidrome on a real iPod.

Sync songs, albums, artists and playlists from your server to a Rockbox iPod from a normal Mac app. Your server transcodes, artwork is re-encoded for old hardware, plays on the device sync back, and two NaviBeat iPod themes are installed. A one time $4.99 unlock. How it works.

The string "rockbox" appears in no other client's source code.

Last.fm

Last.fm, routed through your own server.

Listener counts, similar artists, loved track sync, and scrobbles that go out through Navidrome rather than straight from the app. See the integration.

ListenBrainz

ListenBrainz, first class.

The open, self-hostable alternative from the MusicBrainz nonprofit. Your ListenBrainz shelf, Weekly and Daily Jams, two way loved track sync, and a Preferred Source picker where it shares a surface with Last.fm. See the integration.

NaviBeat Mixes

Mixes your own server builds.

A free, open source plugin for Navidrome 0.63.1 and newer that generates up to 23 playlists from how you listen: morning and night mixes, Rediscover, artist and genre radios, decade mixes. They are ordinary server playlists, so every client sees them. Get the plugin.

The part that does not change

Privacy, security and languages.

Privacy

Zero analytics. Zero tracking.

There is no NaviBeat backend. The app talks to your server and to nothing else, unless you connect Last.fm or ListenBrainz yourself. No telemetry, no crash pings, no accounts. Privacy policy.

Security

mTLS client certificates.

Import a client certificate and reach a server that refuses anyone without one. No other Apple client here supports them at all. Custom proxy headers ride alongside, for setups behind an identity-aware proxy.

Languages

Eleven languages, and you can force one.

English, German, French, Spanish, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Ukrainian, Serbian, Simplified and Traditional Chinese. An in-app picker overrides the system language, including on Apple TV. Ask for yours.

Straight answers

Questions people ask before buying.

Do I need my own server to use NaviBeat?

Yes. NaviBeat is a client, not a service. You run Navidrome, Airsonic-Advanced, Gonic or another OpenSubsonic server, and NaviBeat plays from it. Navidrome is free and open source and sets up in about fifteen minutes with Docker on a Raspberry Pi, a NAS or any spare machine.

Does one purchase cover all the platforms?

Yes. $5.99 once, and it installs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV and Apple Watch through Apple's Universal Purchase. There is no subscription and no per-device charge. The only extra is NaviSyncRock, a separate $4.99 unlock on the Mac for Rockbox iPod sync.

Does NaviBeat work offline?

Yes, thoroughly. Pin songs, albums, playlists or whole artists. Artwork and metadata come down with the audio, so the library browses offline as well as plays. An LRU cache with a size limit you set manages the space, and Prefer Downloaded Music plays the local copy even when you are online.

Which Apple platforms does NaviBeat support?

All five: iPhone on iOS 18 and later, iPad on iPadOS 18, Mac on macOS 15, Apple TV on tvOS 18 and Apple Watch on watchOS 11, plus CarPlay. Among the clients listed in the Navidrome apps directory, NaviBeat is the only one with a native app on all five.

Does NaviBeat track me or collect data?

No. There is no NaviBeat backend and no analytics of any kind. The app connects to your server, and to Last.fm or ListenBrainz only if you set those up yourself. Scrobbles are routed through your own Navidrome rather than sent from the app.

Can I try it before paying?

On the Mac there is a 7 day free trial. On every platform the TestFlight beta is free and open while you are in it, so you can put your own library in front of it before buying. Join the beta.

Everything NaviBeat does.

NaviBeat is a native music player for your own Navidrome or Subsonic server, on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV and Apple Watch, plus CarPlay, for one $5.99 purchase. This is the whole list, and all of it is on the App Store today in 1.4.

New in 1.5.4 / Library Doctor

Library Doctor checks your library.

A new sidebar item reads your library from the server and finds split albums, compilations that are not marked, wrong track numbers, missing covers, damaged tag text and duplicate imports. It names what differs and confirms it in the files. You review every change before it is written, NaviBeat asks Navidrome to scan, and every write can be undone from Write History. The check and Undo are free; writing a fix is a one-time unlock.

Mac. In 1.5.4, now in App Store review, and in the beta.

New in 1.4 / Offline

Offline stopped being a separate mode.

Downloaded music lives inside the normal app. The same Home, the same artists, the same albums, with anything you do not have on the device shown greyed out instead of hidden, so you always know what is missing rather than wondering where it went.

When your server cannot be reached, NaviBeat switches to your downloads on its own and switches back when the server returns. Favourites, ratings and artwork keep working while you are cut off, and catch up afterwards.

New in 1.4 / Player

Jump buttons, either side of Play.

Skip back and forward by 10, 15 or 30 seconds. Look at the row in the screenshot: the jump pair sits either side of Play and Next and Previous are still there, which is the part other players get wrong.

They can be limited to one library, which is the point if your audiobooks sit on the same server as your music: turn them on there and your music player is unchanged. Off until you ask for them.

iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV.

New in 1.4 / Appearance

Pick your own accent colour.

Six one-tap presets, a full colour picker, and a hex field if you know exactly what you want. Amber is still the default and one tap brings it back.

The text and glyphs drawn on top of your colour switch between black and white on their own, so a pale yellow stays as readable as a deep purple, and the gradient follows your choice instead of staying orange.

New in 1.4 / Continuity

Start on one device, carry on with another.

That card at the top of Home is another device playing right now. It names the device, shows the track and how far in it is, and one tap picks the whole queue up where it stands. Tap it and the other device pauses itself, so you are never chased through the house by your own music.

It runs through your own server's play queue, so there is nothing extra to configure and nothing leaves your network.

New in 1.4 / Speakers

Play to the speaker in the room.

Send your music to a UPnP or DLNA device on your network: a network streamer, an AV receiver, a smart television. Your phone hands the music over, goes quiet, and becomes the remote: play, pause, skip, position and volume, and changing track on the phone moves the speaker with it.

The speaker fetches the audio from your own server, so nothing goes through anybody's cloud. NaviBeat asks each device what it can actually decode and sends a format it accepts, rather than assuming. Speakers that announce themselves turn up in the search, and any other is added by typing its address, which is one line out of your router's device list.

New in 1.4 / Playlists

Smart Playlists you write yourself.

Build a playlist out of rules rather than by hand: artist, genre, year, rating, play count, when you added it, whether it is in another playlist, and more. Results update as your library does, and you see them while you are still editing the rules. Definitions sync across your devices, and a snapshot can be written back to your server as an ordinary playlist so every other client sees it too.

New in 1.4 / Servers

Saved servers, and no retyping.

Keep several servers side by side and switch between them without entering anything again. Each entry carries its own proxy headers and its own mTLS client certificate, so a server behind an identity-aware proxy is a saved entry like any other.

New in 1.4 / Artists

A discography that behaves like a discography.

Albums grouped by release type, so albums, EPs, singles, live records and compilations stop fighting for the same list. An All Songs view flattens an artist's whole catalogue into one sortable list, and albums released in the same year are finally in the right order: if your files carry a release date, NaviBeat reads it down to the day, and an original release beats a later reissue.

New in 1.4 / Lyrics

An optional lyrics lookup, off until you ask.

If your server and its own lyrics plugin both come up empty, NaviBeat can ask lrclib.net instead. That lookup is off until you turn it on, and it sends the song title, artist, album and length. Nothing about you and nothing about your server.

New in 1.4 / Automation

Shortcuts, sharing and accessibility.

Shortcuts actions for play, pause, next, previous and shuffle, plus Play Album with an album picker and a shuffle flag, and Play Playlist by name. The album link works outside Shortcuts too, so an NFC sticker on a shelf can start the record next to it. Copy Share Link asks your server for a proper share URL rather than pasting anything of yours into a message. Every transport control announces what it does to VoiceOver, and Reduce Motion turns off the animation the rest of the app leans on. Play Album and the album link are iPhone, iPad and Mac; the transport actions and Play Playlist also reach Apple TV.

New in 1.4 / Search

Search that reaches into lyrics.

Type a line you remember and NaviBeat finds the song, showing the lyric that matched under the title. The scope is narrow and worth stating: it works over songs you have downloaded, because a Navidrome search index does not hold lyrics and no client can ask it for them. iPhone and Mac.

New in 1.4 / Spotlight

Your library in the system search bar.

Find in Spotlight puts your artists and albums into the system index, and a result opens straight into NaviBeat. Off until you switch it on, scoped to your downloads by default and widenable to the whole library, and switching it off deletes everything it wrote. iPhone, iPad and Mac.

New in 1.4 / Speakers

Play to a speaker on your network.

Hand a track to a UPnP or DLNA speaker or receiver on your own network, with transport, position and volume from the app, format negotiation and a saved device list you can edit. Adding one by address is the path that always works, including Denon and Marantz receivers; automatic discovery depends on what a given box announces and on a multicast permission Apple has not granted, so reach for the address field first. iPhone and Mac.

New in 1.4 / Crossfade

One song fades into the next.

Set the overlap and NaviBeat crosses from one track into the other. It works for downloaded files and, now, for streamed songs as well, once the whole outgoing track has arrived on the device. iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV.

New in 1.4 / Browsing

Appears On, a genre tree, and labels you can follow.

An artist page carries an Appears On shelf of albums they only guest on, which Navidrome does not return and the app works out itself. Genres can be built as a tree from your own tags rather than a flat list. A record label on an album page is a link to the rest of that label, and so is the genre beside it. An E badge marks explicit tracks, on rows, on album headers, in the car and on the lock screen.

New in 1.4 / Settings

Seven groups, and a search field.

Settings is no longer one long scroll: Server & Library, Playback & Audio, Appearance & Language, Scrobbling & Stats, Devices, Downloads & Storage and About, each drilling in to its own screen. There is a search field at the top, so a row like Gapless playback or ReplayGain is one typed word away, and it knows a few synonyms: type bit perfect and it finds Direct Playback.

New in 1.4 / Playlists

Playlist folders, and covers that move.

Group playlists into folders so a few hundred of them stop being one flat list. A playlist whose cover is an animated GIF plays it rather than showing a still frame. And Get Info on a song or album shows the file, the tags and the technical detail your server holds.

New in 1.4 / Security

Turn off credential sync without turning off iCloud.

Your server password rides iCloud Keychain by default, because that is how the Apple Watch inherits your pairing without you typing on a watch. There is now a switch for it: Sync credentials to your other Apple devices, under Settings, Server & Library, Security & Sync. Off means the password stays on that one device. An imported mTLS certificate never syncs at all.

New in 1.4 / ListenBrainz

Point it at your own instance.

If you run a ListenBrainz-compatible server, NaviBeat can use it instead of listenbrainz.org: an address, a token and your name on that instance, on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV. Because ListenBrainz Labs is a separate public host, similar-artist enrichment steps back on a self-hosted instance rather than quietly asking the public service about your listening.

New in 1.4 / Quality

The AAC rungs, and letting the server decide.

The quality ladder is nine tiers on every device, and fifteen where Opus plays: Original, AAC 320 down to 96 and MP3 320 down to 128 everywhere, plus Opus 320 down to 64 on the built-in playback engine. AAC is the codec every Apple device decodes natively, which is why the low end of the watch's ladder is AAC rather than Opus. Server decides sends no format and no bitrate at all, so the per-player transcoding profile you configured in Navidrome is what wins.

New in 1.4 / Playback

Lossless that starts when you press play.

Streaming an original FLAC used to mean waiting while the app read through the file to work out how long it was. It now reads the exact length out of the first 42 bytes instead. On an eight minute 24-bit track over Wi-Fi that took the wait from 31 seconds down to 3, and the end of the track is still accurate to the sample. The joins between tracks on a seamless album got the same attention: the player no longer rebuilds its own queue at the exact moment it needs the next song.

New / Classical

Classical, done right.

Classical albums group by work, with every movement listed underneath in the right order. A suite stays one work with four movements, not four unrelated tracks. Straight answers

Questions people ask before buying.

Do I need my own server to use NaviBeat?

Yes. NaviBeat is a client, not a service. You run Navidrome, Airsonic-Advanced, Gonic or another OpenSubsonic server, and NaviBeat plays from it. Navidrome is free and open source and sets up in about fifteen minutes with Docker on a Raspberry Pi, a NAS or any spare machine.

Does one purchase cover all the platforms?

Yes. $5.99 once, and it installs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV and Apple Watch through Apple's Universal Purchase. There is no subscription and no per-device charge. The only extra is NaviSyncRock, a separate $4.99 unlock on the Mac for Rockbox iPod sync.

Does NaviBeat work offline?

Yes, thoroughly. Pin songs, albums, playlists or whole artists. Artwork and metadata come down with the audio, so the library browses offline as well as plays. An LRU cache with a size limit you set manages the space, and Prefer Downloaded Music plays the local copy even when you are online.

Which Apple platforms does NaviBeat support?

All five: iPhone on iOS 17 and later, iPad on iPadOS 17, Mac on macOS 15, Apple TV on tvOS 18 and Apple Watch on watchOS 10, plus CarPlay. Among the clients listed in the Navidrome apps directory, NaviBeat is the only one with a native app on all five.

Does NaviBeat track me or collect data?

No. There is no NaviBeat backend and no analytics of any kind. The app connects to your server, and to Last.fm or ListenBrainz only if you set those up yourself. Scrobbles are routed through your own Navidrome rather than sent from the app.
